The loop

The board is a Python file, board.py. You say what you want, and each change goes once round this ring.

  1. You describe the board

    Say what it is for on the Brief, with cards for its parts, power and decisions. On the board, point at a part or draw where it should go. Each mark can carry a note.

  2. Claude Code edits board.py

    Your marks go with your message as exact references: the part, the pad, the net. Through pino's tools, Claude looks at the board, measures it and reads the checks.

  3. The engine builds and checks it

    On save, it runs the file, places the parts and writes a KiCad 10 project. Then KiCad's electrical and design rule checks run, and pino's own.

  4. The router lays the copper

    When you or Claude ask, KiCadRoutingTools routes the board in the background, and the checks run again on its copper.

  5. pino draws it and tells Claude

    The Schematic, the Board and 3D show the new build. Claude is told what changed and what failed.

What it is for

Small boards

Two or four layers, made for up to about a hundred parts.

JLCPCB parts

Name a part by its LCSC number. pino looks up its stock, its price, and whether JLC calls it basic or extended.

Fab files

Gerbers, drill files, a BOM and a CPL (placement) file, which you upload to JLCPCB yourself. Check every part in JLC's placement preview before you pay.

Linux

pino runs on Linux only. It is developed on Arch with Wayland.

What you need and how to run it

You need

  • Linux
  • Node.js 22.12 or later, and pnpm
  • uv, which runs the engine
  • KiCad 10 with its libraries. pino builds from its symbols and footprints, and its kicad-cli runs the checks
  • Claude Code on your PATH, logged in

Then

git clone https://github.com/luisg0nc/pino.git
cd pino
pnpm install
scripts/pino ~/boards

pino checks what it needs on its first screen and says what is missing. The first route installs the router into a cache in your home folder, once.
